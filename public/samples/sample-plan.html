Strand 1 · Direct · Relational warmth

Commercial Finance Consultancy

Project-based commercial finance for PE-backed mid-market businesses. The Direct outreach strand — first sales conversations come from people who already know you.

Market snapshot — London PE-backed mid-market

The buyer is a CFO or FD at a £20M–£150M PE-backed business, typically accounting-trained and weak on commercial narrative. The trigger moment is a board paper requiring rigorous commercial defence on a tight timeline. Day rates above £950 are achievable from your background; starting below £700 misaligns expectations. First 3–4 engagements come through personal referrals — inbound from cold LinkedIn comes later, after a visible track record exists.

Why Direct first

Your network composition (Q11, Q13) is unusually strong for this segment. ~25 Big 4 alumni now in CFO/FD roles at mid-market businesses. ~12 PE-backed company CFOs who would take your call, of which 8 are people you've worked with directly. That's not a network you build through visibility or community — it's a network you activate through Direct contact, one warm message at a time.

Strand 2 · Visibility · Content-led entry

Productized Investment Case Development

A 10-day fixed-scope sprint that produces a board-ready investment case. The Visibility strand — the audience finds you, not the other way around.

Market snapshot — LinkedIn finance leadership audience

Your audience is mid-market and PE-backed CFOs, finance directors, and corporate development leads. The right posting cadence is 2× per week, Tuesday and Thursday morning UK. Engagement compounds slowly — your first six posts will get less than your sixteenth. Don't read engagement against vanity benchmarks; read it against whether the right people are commenting. One substantive comment from a PE-backed CFO is worth fifty likes from peers.

Why Visibility second

You have raw material no one else has — three years of private writing about commercial finance. The Visibility strand is a low-cost surface that compounds: every post is a permanent, searchable artefact that demonstrates judgment. It also feeds Strand 1 — a CFO you reconnect with in week two will check your LinkedIn. A live, sharp profile turns a polite "let's catch up" into a real conversation about the work.

Strand 3 · Community · Slow-build presence

Coaching for Mid-Career Women in Finance

A coaching practice anchored in your Women in Finance mentoring track record. The Community strand — earning standing inside a real network before any commercial conversation.

Market snapshot — UK Women in Finance communities

Three named communities are the right ones for someone with your background. The buyer for paid coaching is mid-career women in finance approaching promotion to Director or Head of, where the gap is rarely technical and almost always executive presence and board-readiness. Rates for senior 1:1 coaching from credentialed practitioners run £200–£400 per session, six-session blocks. Community traction is slow by design — three months of consistent presence usually precedes the first paid enquiry.

Why Community third

You have a real, named track record (four mid-career women through promotion in 24 months) that gives you legitimate standing in this space. The hesitation in your profile (Q10, "the idea of positioning myself to strangers makes me uncomfortable") is the exact reason Community is the right entry mode for you. Community lets you become known through contribution before you ever sell anything — it's the mode that fits your temperament.

Template 02

LinkedIn DM — second-tier contact, less recent relationship

When you've worked together briefly or know each other through a mutual connection but the relationship has gone quiet.

Hi [Name] — hope you're well. I've been following your move to [company] and the [specific thing] looked interesting. Quick context: I'm starting to do some independent commercial finance work alongside my role — primarily for PE-backed businesses needing senior commercial finance support on specific decisions (investment cases, board prep, commercial reviews). Sarah
Template 03

Referral ask — to a former colleague who said yes to a coffee

After your reconnect call has gone well and they've expressed interest in what you're doing — this is the natural follow-up that turns a coffee into a referral.

[Name] — thanks for the time on Tuesday, that was useful. Quick follow-up: you mentioned that [specific thing they said about how their finance function works / what their board needs / what they'd want from an external partner]. That's exactly the kind of conversation I want to be having more of. Is there anyone you'd recommend I speak to? I'm specifically looking for CFOs or FDs at PE-backed businesses £20M–£150M who'd value 20 minutes of an exploratory conversation about commercial finance support — same diagnostic frame I used with you, no pitch. Two or three names would be enormously helpful. Happy to send a draft message you can forward, or to reach out cold with you copied. Sarah
Template 04

Verbal positioning statement — the 30-second version

For when someone asks "what are you doing now?" — at an industry event, on a coffee, in a passing conversation. Practise it until it sounds unhedged.

"I'm working with mid-market and PE-backed businesses on commercial finance — business cases, investment decisions, board-level financial analysis. The thing I'm best at is defending the numbers in a hostile commercial conversation, which is what most PE-backed boards actually need. Right now it's alongside my day job, but I'm building toward making it my main thing." — Practise saying it without qualifications. No "trying to" or "sort of" or "if it works out". Say it as if it's already true, because it is.
